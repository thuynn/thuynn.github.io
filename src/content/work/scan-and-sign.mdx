## The problem

Delivery staff need to confirm hundreds of deliveries a day, often outdoors, with one hand, in a hurry. Every extra
tap or ambiguous state costs time and causes disputes about whether a parcel was delivered.

## Flow

1. **Authenticate** by scanning a personal QR code — no passwords on shared devices.
2. **Dashboard** shows today's progress (delivered vs. not delivered) and one primary action: start a scan.
3. **Scan the barcode** inside a high-contrast frame; a clear error appears if no shipment matches.
4. **Package detail** shows tracking number, scan time and current location.
5. **Confirm** with a digital signature or a photo of the signed document — or
6. **Report a failed delivery** with quick reasons: can't find the address, no name on the doorbell, nobody at
   home, or other.

## Design decisions

- One primary action per screen, sized for thumbs and gloves.
- Success and failure paths are equally short — reporting a problem shouldn't feel like a punishment.
- Every confirmation shows tracking number, time and address, so the record is self-explanatory.
